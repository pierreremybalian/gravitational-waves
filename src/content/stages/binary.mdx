A pair of masses in orbit is the simplest thing that makes gravitational waves: a changing quadrupole, mass distributed unevenly and turning. A spinning ball radiates nothing; a dumbbell does. Two black holes are the most compact dumbbell nature makes, so they can orbit fast and close and radiate enormously.

Because the pattern repeats every half orbit, the gravitational-wave frequency is exactly twice the orbital frequency. When LIGO measures a 35 Hz wave, the black holes are going round 17.5 times a second.
