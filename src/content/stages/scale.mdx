A strain of 10⁻²¹ is like measuring the distance to the nearest star, Proxima Centauri, and noticing it change by the width of a hair. No ruler can do it, and nothing in the detector can be allowed to move by even that much for any other reason: the mirrors hang from four-stage pendulums on seismically isolated platforms, inside one of the largest ultra-high vacuums on Earth.

The trick is light. A laser beam is split, sent down both arms, bounced back and forth hundreds of times, and recombined. A change in the arms’ difference shifts how the two beams interfere, and that is what the detector reads.
