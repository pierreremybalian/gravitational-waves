Measuring a length to one part in 10²¹ needs a ruler that does not itself change. Light is that ruler: its speed is fixed, so a change in an arm’s length becomes a change in the time the light takes, which becomes a shift in where its waves’ crests fall when the two beams are recombined.

The L shape matters. A gravitational wave arriving from overhead stretches space along one direction while squeezing it along the perpendicular one, so one arm gets longer exactly as the other gets shorter. Comparing two arms doubles the signal and cancels anything that changes both the same way, like the laser’s own wavelength wandering.

The drawing has three exaggerations, all stated on screen. The mirrors move nearly a hundred billion billion times more than they did; the light’s wavelength is drawn as a third of a scene unit instead of a millionth of a metre; and the swing of the arms is slowed twelve times so the eye can follow what the ear hears at full speed.
