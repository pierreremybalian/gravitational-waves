The catalogue is a census of the dark. Before 2015 the only black holes with measured masses were in X-ray binaries in our own galaxy, all under about 20 suns. The mergers revealed a population of heavier ones, 30 suns and more, and a few, like GW190521 and GW231123, so heavy they are hard to make from a single dying star.

Neutron stars are rarer here because they are lighter and quieter: their chirps are fainter, so they are heard only from closer. GW170817, at 40 megaparsecs, is still the only one seen in light as well.

The newest events come from the fourth observing run, which ended in 2025; their full parameters are still being published. Their hollow beads will fill in.
