Everything shakes the mirrors. The ground moves by about a micrometre at a few hertz, hundreds of billions of times more than the arm change of the signal; several stages of active and passive isolation take most of that out, and what is left still dominates below 20 hertz. Atoms in the mirror coatings and their suspension fibres jiggle with heat. And light arrives in photons, at random moments, so the measured brightness flickers by itself: shot noise, the quantum floor at high frequencies.

None of this can be removed completely. What makes detection possible is that the noise is understood: its spectrum is measured continuously, and the signal has a shape that the noise does not.
