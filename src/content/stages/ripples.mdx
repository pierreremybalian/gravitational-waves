A gravitational wave is transverse: it stretches and squeezes space across its direction of travel, never along it. And it is a quadrupole wave: one axis stretches while the perpendicular one squeezes, so the area of the ring stays the same. That is why LIGO is built as an L: one arm lengthens while the other shortens, and the detector measures the difference.

Two independent patterns exist, labelled + and ×, rotated 45 degrees from each other. The mix a detector receives depends on how the source is oriented; a binary seen face-on sends both, a quarter cycle apart, so the squeeze rotates with the orbit.
