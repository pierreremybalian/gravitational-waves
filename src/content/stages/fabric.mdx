General relativity replaces the force of gravity with geometry. Matter tells spacetime how to curve; spacetime tells matter how to move. In a weak field, like the Sun’s, the theory reduces to Newton’s law with small corrections, and those corrections are what the eclipse expeditions of 1919 went looking for.

Einstein showed in 1916 that the equations also allow waves: changes in curvature that travel at the speed of light, carrying energy away from their source. He doubted they could ever be detected. The effect on anything here is unimaginably small, as the rest of this explanation shows.
