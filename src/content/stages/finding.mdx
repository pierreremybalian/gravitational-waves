A matched filter works because a chirp is not a random burst: it has a precise shape, set by the masses and spins of the two bodies, and that shape is calculable in advance. Multiplying the data by the expected shape and adding everything up makes the parts that match add together, while noise, which does not know the shape, mostly cancels itself out.

The real searches do not know the masses beforehand. They run the data through a bank of hundreds of thousands of templates covering every plausible pair, and keep the best match. GW150914 was found within three minutes of reaching the detectors by an unmodelled burst search, then confirmed by two template searches.
