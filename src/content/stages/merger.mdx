No formula describes the merger itself: it is computed by solving Einstein’s equations on supercomputers, and the results are folded into the waveform models that LIGO fits to its data. Before the merger, the signal is fixed by the masses and spins; after it, by the remnant alone. A black hole has no surface and no structure, so the ringing is set entirely by its mass and spin. The frequency and decay time measured in GW150914’s ringdown agree with what general relativity predicts for a black hole of about 62 solar masses spinning at 0.7 of the maximum.

At its peak the collision radiated more power than all the light from all the stars in the observable universe, yet its effect at Earth, 1.3 billion light years away, was a stretch of one part in 10²¹.
