Two detectors are the minimum for a claim. A truck, a lightning strike or a glitch in the electronics can shake one site; nothing local shakes both within ten milliseconds with the same waveform. In the months around GW150914 the collaboration measured how often that happens by chance by sliding one detector’s data against the other’s by more than the light-travel time, millions of times: never as loud as this.

The timing also points. With two sites the source is somewhere on a ring; with three (Virgo joined in August 2017) the rings cross, and the sky area shrinks from hundreds of square degrees to tens. That is what let telescopes find the kilonova of GW170817 within eleven hours.
